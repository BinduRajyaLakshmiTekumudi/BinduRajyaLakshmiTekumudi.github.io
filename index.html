What's on My Plate?

Mobile Computer Vision for Food-on-Plate Recognition

Listen to this page

Imagine you are blind or have low vision, and a plate is set in front of you. What is on it? Where is each item? How much is there? Today you would have to ask someone or touch the food to find out. This tutorial reviews how a phone camera and computer vision try to answer one question: what's on my plate, where is it, and how much is there?

Phone says: "Rice at 9 o'clock. Chicken at 12. Salad at 3."

About this tutorial

This tutorial focuses on blind and low-vision (LV) people, and is written for anyone new to food recognition with computer vision. I reviewed recent research from the last two years on how a phone camera can recognize the food on a plate. By the end, you should be able to explain the three main steps these systems use: finding each food, naming it, and outlining it to measure the portion. You will also see why overlapping food, mixed dishes, and sauces are still hard to handle, and how classic CNN models compare with newer vision-language models.

The tutorial takes about 20 to 25 minutes. Each page has a short audio narration at the top, and there is a quiz at the end so you can check what you learned.
