Annotated bibliography

References in order of first appearance. Papers from 2010 to 2021 are used for background only; the main review relies on work from the last two years. Links to software documentation and product pages are included where the tutorial uses them.

Listen to this page

[1] Adams, S. (2020, August 11). Use Lookout to take care of daily tasks faster. The Keyword (Google blog). Link

Synopsis: Announces Lookout's Food Label mode, which identifies packaged foods by label or barcode and guides blind users to position the item, plus a redesign based on feedback from blind and low-vision users.

Reliability: Official Google blog post by a product manager on the accessibility team. Reliable for describing the product, but promotional and not research. Older (2020), used only to describe an existing app.

[2] Zahra, M. M. F. (2025). Enhancing accessibility for visually impaired individuals. University of Colombo School of Computing. Link

Synopsis: A thesis that builds a mobile app for visually impaired users: it detects food in a video of the meal with YOLOv8 running as TensorFlow Lite on a mid-range Android phone, and speaks the detected foods with text-to-speech. It was evaluated with visually impaired participants.

Reliability: Student thesis, not peer reviewed. Useful as a recent, realistic LV system example; results should be treated with caution.

[3] Zhu, F., Bosch, M., Woo, I., Kim, S., Boushey, C. J., Ebert, D. S., & Delp, E. J. (2010). The use of mobile devices in aiding dietary assessment and evaluation. IEEE Journal of Selected Topics in Signal Processing, 4(4), 756–766.

Synopsis: Background paper. Introduces Purdue's TADA system, which replaces a written food diary with a photo of the meal.

Reliability: Peer-reviewed IEEE journal from an established Purdue lab. Older (2010), used only for historical background.

[4] Chrintz-Gath, G., Daivadanam, M., Matta, L., & McKeever, S. (2025). Image-based dietary assessment using the Swedish plate model: Evaluation of deep learning-based You Only Look Once (YOLO) models. JMIR Formative Research, 9, e70124. https://doi.org/10.2196/70124 Link

Synopsis: Compares YOLOv7, YOLOv8, and YOLOv9 for detecting food components in meal photos and checking their proportions against the Swedish plate model. Uses preprocessing and augmentation and reports precision, recall, mAP, and F1; YOLOv8 performed best on peak precision and F1.

Reliability: Peer-reviewed open-access medical informatics journal; authors from academic institutions. Recent and directly relevant.

[5] Ji, X., Feng, Y., Lu, H., Chu, D., & Han, Q. (2026). Image-based prediction of food weight and nutritional composition in bowl-served meals using semantic segmentation and multi-view 3D reconstruction. Nutrients, 18(13), 2119. https://doi.org/10.3390/nu18132119 Link

Synopsis: Predicts the weight and nutrients of bowl meals by combining an improved segmentation network, multi-view 3D reconstruction, and a layered compensation strategy for staple food hidden under toppings. Whole-bowl errors were about 13% for calories and 11% for total food mass.

Reliability: Peer-reviewed MDPI journal in nutrition science. Recent and directly relevant to occlusion.

[6] Thames, Q., Karpur, A., Norris, W., Xia, F., Panait, L., Weyand, T., & Sim, J. (2021). Nutrition5k: Towards automatic nutritional understanding of generic food. CVPR 2021.

Synopsis: Background paper. A dataset of real cafeteria dishes with overhead RGB-D images and measured nutrition.

Reliability: Top-tier conference, Google Research. Background only.

[7] Mu, Y., Sun, J., & He, J. (2025). Benchmarking and improving foundation model dietary estimates from meal images. Proceedings of the 16th ACM International Conference on Bioinformatics, Computational Biology, and Health Informatics (BCB 2025). https://doi.org/10.1145/3765612.3767255 Link

Synopsis: Compares seven large multimodal models (GPT, Gemini, Llama) with an RGB-D model on Nutrition5k and phone photos. Feeding a predicted food weight to Gemini 2.5 Flash lowered its carbohydrate error from 56.6% to 39.5% on 78 cases, and giving the true weight lowered it further to 20.2%.

Reliability: Peer-reviewed ACM conference paper from Old Dominion University. Small test set (78 cases).

[8] Bossard, L., Guillaumin, M., & Van Gool, L. (2014). Food-101: Mining discriminative components with random forests. ECCV 2014. Springer.

Synopsis: Background paper. Introduces the Food-101 dataset of 101 food classes that many later classifiers train on.

Reliability: Top-tier computer vision conference. Older (2014), used only for background.

[9] Min, W., Jiang, S., Liu, L., Rui, Y., & Jain, R. (2019). A survey on food computing. ACM Computing Surveys, 52(5), Article 92.

Synopsis: Background survey of food computing tasks, datasets, and methods.

Reliability: Peer-reviewed ACM survey. Older (2019), background only.

[10] Romero-Tapiador, S., Tolosana, R., Lacruz-Pleguezuelos, B., Marcos Zambrano, L. J., Bazán, G. X., Espinosa-Salinas, I., Fierrez, J., Ortega-Garcia, J., Carrillo de Santa Pau, E., & Morales, A. (2025). Are vision-language models ready for dietary assessment? Exploring the next frontier in AI-powered food image recognition. CVPR 2025 Workshops. Link

Synopsis: Tests six VLMs (ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude, Moondream, DeepSeek, LLaVA) on FoodNExTDB, 9,263 expert-labeled images. Closed-source models did best, with over 90% expert-weighted recall on single products, but struggled with cooking styles and look-alike foods.

Reliability: Published at a CVPR 2025 workshop by Universidad Autonoma de Madrid researchers. Recent and reliable.

[11] Hosseinian, A., Dehghani Zahedani, A., Mansoor, U., Hashemi, N., & Woodward, M. (2025). January Food Benchmark (JFB): A public benchmark dataset and evaluation suite for multimodal food analysis. arXiv:2508.09966. Link

Synopsis: Releases 1,000 human-validated food images and a scoring framework. Their specialized model scored 86.2 overall versus 74.1 for the best general VLM setup (GPT-4o).

Reliability: arXiv preprint from a company (January AI), which also built the winning model, so possible bias.

[12] TensorFlow. (n.d.). Transfer learning and fine-tuning. TensorFlow Core tutorials. Link

Synopsis: Official tutorial showing how to reuse a pretrained MobileNetV2, freeze it, add a new classifier, and fine-tune the top layers. The code on the Pipeline page is adapted from it.

Reliability: Official documentation from Google's TensorFlow team. Reliable for code, not a research paper.

[13] Shrestha, J. (2026). Ingredient-level food image segmentation for nutrition awareness. arXiv:2606.24059. Link

Synopsis: Fine-tunes two small SegFormer models (B0 and B1) on FoodSeg103. The larger B1 reached 0.79 pixel accuracy and 0.32 mean IoU on 2,135 test images, with failures on mixed dishes, thin regions, small ingredients, and unclear boundaries.

Reliability: Single-author arXiv preprint from DePaul University, not yet peer reviewed. Uses small models, so its scores are lower than state-of-the-art, but it is clear about its limitations.

[14] Meyers, A., Johnston, N., Rathod, V., Korattikara, A., Gorban, A., Silberman, N., Guadarrama, S., Papandreou, G., Huang, J., & Murphy, K. (2015). Im2Calories: Towards an automated mobile vision food diary. ICCV 2015.

Synopsis: Background paper. Google's early pipeline that goes from a meal photo to a calorie estimate.

Reliability: Top-tier conference, Google Research. Older (2015), used only for background.

[15] Lv, L., Wu, J., Wu, D., Liu, X., Xia, Y., & He, Y. (2024). Differential evolution integrated hybrid deep learning model for object detection in pre-made dishes. arXiv:2412.20370. Link

Synopsis: Combines YOLOv5, YOLOv8, and DETR with weights tuned by differential evolution and weighted boxes fusion. The ensemble reached 90.9% mAP50 on 11 ingredient classes, beating every single model.

Reliability: arXiv preprint from Southwest University and William & Mary. Recent; used for the overlap discussion.

[16] Jin, S., Zhang, J., Zhang, X., Tian, Z., Jiang, F., Yin, G., Lin, W., Liu, Y., & Yan, R. (2026). DiningBench: A hierarchical multi-view benchmark for perception and reasoning in the dietary domain. ACL 2026. Link

Synopsis: A benchmark of 3,021 dishes with about five images each and hard negatives from the same menu. 29 VLMs struggle with fine-grained recognition and nutrition reasoning.

Reliability: Peer-reviewed at ACL 2026, a top language and AI venue. Recent and reliable.
